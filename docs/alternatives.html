WHAT EACH MAP CAN SAY

Different data.
Different answers.

These tools and data sources answer related questions, but their points, estimates and maps are not interchangeable. opencomm is independent of them.

CELL INVENTORY

Where might a cell be?

OpenCellID provides estimated cell locations, not measured signal strength or verified tower positions. CellMapper crowdsources tower and signal mapping. Unwired Labs LocationAPI uses cell identifiers for device geolocation. Mozilla Location Service retired its third-party service and cell exports in 2024; it is historical context, not a current open feed.

EXPERIENCE MAPS

What did users experience?

nPerf and Opensignal use user-contributed tests to show mobile experience. Ookla Speedtest Maps shows network coverage in its app. These are not source datasets used by opencomm; availability, tests and measurement methods differ.

REGULATORS & RESEARCH

Measured, but sampled.

Ofcom Map Your Mobile is a UK network checker; its separately published vehicle drive-test measurements are not yet loaded here. Germany's Bundesnetzagentur Mobilfunk-Check reports user tests and technology availability, not an RF-strength substitute. opencomm currently plots sampled CC BY 4.0 TU Dortmund DoNext signal measurements along one H-Bahn route.

ISRAEL

Permits are not reception.

Israel's active cellular antenna map shows regulatory installation records, not a user's signal at each point. Infrastructure includes Pelephone, Cellcom and PHI (the Partner and HOT shared network), as described in Knesset research. No Israeli measurements or OpenCellID export have been loaded into this demo. Do not treat antenna permits, estimated cells, and RF readings as equivalent.

What makes opencomm different today: a tiny, reproducible public extract with each point labeled by evidence grade, source, license and measurement. It does not claim full-country coverage, live network quality, or superiority to these services.
